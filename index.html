Following my input on Decision 2 (items 4, 10, 12), here is a concrete example of what item #10 (machine-readable WDS) could look like in practice, built on the WERA vocabulary introduced in PR #21 (descriptor schema and registry codes).
The workflow: the WG's recurring invoice-processing example, composed with the human-approval-gate pattern (scope D, human-in-the-loop) and an audit system of record (scope B). The design deliberately makes approval, receipt, and audit events first-class machine-readable artifacts, so the evidence record is derived from the same source of truth as the execution (INV-012, INV-013, INV-014, INV-017).

Three observations this example surfaces:
1 The descriptor is the source of truth. Human-readable artifacts (rationale, diagram, review material, implementation handoff) are generated views of it, not separately maintained documents. That is the portability principle from my earlier comment made concrete.
2 A protected effect (EF4) with no receipt, no audit record, and no trace is not verifiable. Making RCP, AUD, and TRC mandatory primitives keeps the descriptor honest about evidence: what was approved, what was executed, and what was recorded all derive from one canonical design.
3 INV-013 (approval binds to an exact version) is what makes human-in-the-loop auditable: the executed payment is provably the one the human approved, because the approval references the same proposal version the executor consumed.
